# Himali Tea House: Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: Are Himali teas certified organic?
Yes. Our primary high-altitude partner gardens in Illam and Dhankuta operate under organic certification standards (USDA Organic and EU Bio equivalent), adhering strictly to zero chemical pesticides, synthetic fungicides, or artificial fertilizers.

### Q2: What is the caffeine content across different tea types?
White teas like Silver Needle possess gentle caffeine levels (approx 15-20mg per cup) with calming L-theanine. Green teas provide balanced alertness (25-35mg per cup). Oolong teas average 35-45mg per cup, while orthodox Golden Black teas contain the highest caffeine levels (45-60mg per cup), ideal for morning energizing.

### Q3: How should I store loose-leaf tea to retain freshness?
Store tea leaves in an airtight, opaque container away from sunlight, heat, humidity, and strong spices. Never store tea in the refrigerator or freezer, as moisture condensation degrades delicate essential aromatic oils.

### Q4: What is the shelf life of Himali loose-leaf teas?
When stored properly in our airtight caddies:
- Green and White teas remain peak fresh for 18 months.
- Black and Oolong teas stay vibrant for 24 to 36 months.
- Aged Roasted Oolongs improve in depth for up to 5 years.

### Q8: What is the difference between First Flush and Second Flush teas?
First Flush teas are harvested between late March and April right after winter dormancy; they produce light, delicate, floral, and brisk liquor with Muscatel notes. Second Flush is harvested in May and June; the leaves yield a deeper amber body, richer sweetness, and well-rounded fruity complexity.

### Q10: How do I brew tea using a traditional Gaiwan?
Preheat your porcelain gaiwan with hot water, discard the rinse, add 4 to 5 grams of leaves, pour water at the recommended temperature, and steep for short bursts (15 to 30 seconds for steep 1, adding 5 seconds for subsequent infusions). High-grade orthodox teas yield 5 to 7 satisfying steepings.

### Q11: Can I make cold brew with Himalayan teas?
Absolutely. High Mountain Oolongs, Green Pearl, and White Peony excel in cold brewing. Use 10 grams of tea leaves per liter of cold filtered water and steep in the refrigerator for 8 to 12 hours. Cold brewing extracts natural sweetness with zero astringency.
